How to use Threat Tree Diagrammer

Quick start

  • Drag a CSV file onto the page, or click Load CSV.
  • Click Auto-Arrange for a tidy layout, then drag nodes to fine-tune it.
  • If the diagram is too big for one sheet, click a node and then Split Page.
  • Click Save CSV to keep your work, and Export SVG to get pictures for your document.

Preparing the CSV file

ColumnWhat to enter
IDRequired. A unique name for the node.
Parent_IDThe node's parent. Leave blank for the top node. For up to 3 parents, use quotes: "a, b, c"
TypeRequired. AND or OR for a gate, BASIC for a leaf node.
TitleOne line, up to 24 characters.
DescriptionUp to 4 lines of 25 characters. Line breaks you type in the cell are kept.
PotentialityShown just below the title box, at its right edge.
X, YPosition on the Master tab. If every node is blank or 0, the diagram is arranged for you.

Files saved after a split also contain Page, Page_X, Page_Y, Split_Page, Split_X and Split_Y. The tool fills these in. If you add a row by hand to a split file, give it a Page value.

A file with a problem, such as a duplicate ID, a missing parent or a circular link, won't load. A notice lists what to fix, and any diagram already on screen stays as it was.

Reading the diagram

  • Several parents: a node with more than one parent gets a separate line from each. A parent on the left enters at the top-left, and a parent on the right at the top-right.
  • Dots and crossings: a dot marks a real connection. Lines that cross without a dot aren't connected.
  • Duplicates: nodes with the same Title, Description and Potentiality are duplicates. They share a box colour on screen. Duplicated leaf nodes also share a number in their circle. The match is exact, including capital letters.

Tracing an attack path (Master tab only)

  • Click any node to trace upward from it to the top of the diagram.
  • Solid red is the path. If a node has several parents, all of them are followed.
  • Dashed orange marks the other inputs an AND gate on the path also needs, one level only. An OR gate just passes through.
  • Everything else fades. To clear the trace, click empty space or press Esc.
  • Traces are for the screen only and never appear in exports.

Splitting into pages

  • The Master tab always shows the full diagram. Tabs appear at the bottom left.
  • Click a node, then click Split Page. Everything below it moves to a new page. On a page tab, a click selects the node with a blue outline. On Master, a click also starts a trace.
  • The first split creates page1 (the main diagram) and page2 (the branch).
  • The original node gets a triangle marked with the new page. The node is repeated at the top of the new page, with a triangle linking back. Click a triangle to jump to that page.
  • If a split isn't possible, a notice explains why. The most common reason is that a node being moved also has a parent that would stay behind.
  • Each tab has its own layout. A split leaves gaps on the original page, so click Auto-Arrange there to close them.
  • To reverse a split, open that page's tab and click Undo Split.

Checking printed text size

  • Sizes assume Letter portrait with 1-inch margins. Hover over a tab to see the size in points.
  • All pages print at one shared size, set by the page that has to shrink the most. A message beside the tabs names that page. Master has its own size.
  • A tab turns amber when its text would print below 6 pt. Split or narrow that page to raise the size.
  • As a guide, aim for at most 5 boxes side by side on a page.

Exporting pictures

  • Export SVG downloads the Master diagram and every page in one click, from any tab. Your browser may ask for permission to download several files, so choose Allow.
  • Export PNG downloads only the current tab, and its text size may not match the other pages once placed in a document.
  • Exports always have white boxes and dark lines. Duplicate colours are left out, while leaf numbers and page links are kept.
  • A small line below each exported diagram identifies it, for example Page 2 of 5 · config.csv · 2026-11-06. It shows the page, the CSV file you loaded and the export date.
  • SVG export is turned off in dark mode, so switch to light first.
  • Re-export every page after your last change, and don't resize the pictures in your document, so the text sizes stay matched.

Saving your work

  • Save CSV downloads threat_tree_export.csv, with the positions on every tab and all your splits. Your original file isn't changed. Loading the saved file brings it all back.
  • "Unsaved changes" appears beside the tabs when you've changed the layout or pages since loading or saving.
  • Refreshing the page keeps your diagram, but closing the tab clears it, and nothing is kept on the computer after that. With unsaved changes, your browser warns you first. It shows the same warning for a refresh, which is safe to accept.
  • Loading a file replaces the current diagram, and asks first if you have unsaved changes. After editing a CSV in another program, load it again to see the changes.
  • The tool works offline, and your data never leaves your computer.
